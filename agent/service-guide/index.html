Agent reference — this page is not linked from the website. It is here for the In2Skin phone agent to read and use on calls.

Use this page to match what a caller describes to a treatment, in plain words — then offer to book it: “Let’s go ahead and book that for you.”

The first rule

If a caller describes a skin condition — acne, rosacea, or any skin problem they are being treated for — or if you are unsure about anything, suggest the $30 consultation instead of picking a treatment. Never diagnose. Never promise a cure. Say “helps with”, never “cures”.

The consultation is 20 minutes, $30, first-time only, paid up front by card, credited in full toward the first treatment. Video or in person. The caller gets a recommendation and a booking link, with the $30 already applied.

What callers describe — and what to suggest

Acne-prone skin

Source: the acne-prone skin page. Breakouts don’t need harsh treatment — they need the right treatment. Suggest the $30 consultation — the treatment is shaped around the skin at the visit. What the site says: the Classic Facial is the gentle first step for breakout-prone skin — a thorough deep cleanse, gentle exfoliation, steam and a hydration mask. Microdermabrasion ($55) and Light Therapy ($55) can be added. Chemical peels ($125–$475) are matched to the skin at consultation. Teenagers are welcome, and everything is explained as it happens. Regular visits every 4 to 6 weeks help keep breakouts under control.

Rosacea & sensitive skin

Source: the rosacea and sensitive skin page. Skin that flushes easily needs a gentle hand. Suggest the $30 consultation — the treatment is shaped around the skin at the visit. What the site says: calm, soothing care with gentle products and a soft touch, no harsh scrubs. Red light therapy is a favourite for redness-prone skin — warm, restful light that encourages a calmer, more even-looking complexion. The Classic Facial ($85, 55 minutes) has no downtime, and the esthetician watches how the skin responds at every step and adjusts. Ask the caller to tell the studio about their skin when they book.

Dull skin

Source: the dull skin page. Dull skin is usually tired skin — surface build-up blocking the light, and moisture that can’t get in. Suggest Microdermabrasion, $55 for 20 minutes — the fastest glow, no downtime — or the Classic Facial, $85 for 55 minutes. Also Microneedling / NANO Needling, $65, and Light Therapy, $55. The change is visible the same day, and the glow lasts for days. For a special occasion, suggest microdermabrasion a day or two before.

Fine lines

Source: the fine lines page. Fine lines soften when skin is firm, hydrated and making fresh collagen. Suggest the Anti-Aging Facial, $125 for 80 minutes — firming massage, targeted serums and rich hydration, for firmer, smoother, brighter skin. Add Microneedling / NANO Needling, $65 for 20 minutes — ultra-fine tips help serums absorb more deeply — or Light Therapy, $55. Results build with regular visits every 4 to 6 weeks.

Tired skin

Source: the tired skin page. Tired skin is usually a tired person. Suggest The Works — $160 with a Classic Facial, $195 with an Anti-Aging Facial: facial, red light therapy and reflexology all at once, while the mask works. It is the closest thing to a nap that still counts as skincare, and many clients fall asleep. Or the Classic Facial, $85 for 55 minutes. Reflexology on its own is $35 for 20 minutes — many clients sleep beautifully that night.

What each treatment feels like

Classic Facial — $85, 55 minutes. Warm, gentle and deeply relaxing. Many clients drift off — and wake up glowing.

Anti-Aging Facial — $125, 80 minutes. Eighty unhurried minutes of expert care — a mini holiday for skin and mind.

The Works — $160 / $195. So relaxing, clients fall asleep. Facial, red light and reflexology, all at once.

Chemical Peels — $125–$475, 55 minutes. A gentle warming sensation as it renews, with expert care throughout. The visit starts with a consultation — it is part of the 55 minutes.

Microneedling / NANO Needling — $65, 20 minutes. Light and gentle — a soft gliding sensation while you relax. One treatment; the studio picks the needle size at the visit.

Microdermabrasion — $55, 20 minutes. A gentle polishing massage, finished with soothing hydration.

Light Therapy — $55, 20 minutes. Warm and peaceful. Many clients nap while the light works.

Reflexology — $35, 20 minutes. Gentle, rhythmic pressure — calm spreads through the whole body in minutes.

When to suggest the consultation instead

A skin condition, or skin the caller is being treated for. The caller is unsure which treatment they need. The caller wants a chemical peel — peels are always matched at consultation. The caller asks a medical question, or anything you are unsure about. Say: “A $30 consultation, 20 minutes, video or in person — you get a recommendation and a booking link, with the $30 already applied to your first treatment.”

What never to say

Never diagnose a skin condition. Never say a treatment will cure anything. Never quote a price the site doesn’t list. Never tell a caller where the studio is or how to get there — that information is shared only when they book. Never read out Sarah’s phone number. Full policies are on the policies page; aftercare is on the aftercare page.
